About us

We are a grassroots movement dedicated to ending the exploitation of horses in India's racing industry.

Our mission

Why we exist

To end horseracing in India through advocacy, education, legislative action, and public awareness, creating a future where horses are respected as sentient beings with the right to live free from exploitation.

Our vision

A future where horseracing no longer exists in India. Where every horse lives free from the physical and psychological trauma of racing. Where their inherent value is recognized, and their right to freedom is protected by law and embraced by society.

What we do

  • Document racing deaths and injuries across India
  • Push for legislation to ban horseracing in India
  • Conduct investigative research on industry practices
  • Build public opposition through education and advocacy
  • Honor horses who have died with memorial pages
Why this matters

This is not a sport

Horse racing is not a sport, it's systematic exploitation. Horses don't choose to race. They're bred into captivity, trained through force and fear, and pushed beyond their natural limits for human profit and entertainment.

The industry calls deaths "accidents" and injuries "part of the sport." But when horses die at a rate of hundreds every year across Indian tracks, when catastrophic injuries are routine, when horses race while injured or medicated, this isn't sport. It's abuse, dressed up in tradition and spectacle.

We exist because horses cannot speak for themselves. Because the industry won't stop on its own. Because every horse who dies on a racetrack dies for nothing but human greed and entertainment.

Our approach

How we work

Legislative Action

Urging state and central governments to ban horseracing in India, and calling on chief ministers and legislators to act on the cruelty documented at Indian racetracks.

Investigation

Documenting deaths, exposing conditions, and conducting research that reveals the truth behind racing industry PR.

Advocacy

Building a mass movement through education, memorials, and public campaigns that shift social attitudes toward racing.
